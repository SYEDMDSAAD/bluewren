      How Bluewren works with Alexa+

      1. Bluewren is an MCP server that an airline would run. Alexa+ connects to it over Streamable HTTP (MCP 2025-11-25) and gets 10 tools: trip alerts, my trips, flight status, rebooking options, rebook, refund eligibility, request refund, refund status, meals and hotel, and bag status.
      2. Alexa speaks up first. Alexa subscribes to the bluewren://trips resource. When operations cancel or delay a flight, the server sends notifications/resources/updated, and Alexa calls trip_alerts and tells you with the best next step, before you reach the airport.
      3. Your rights, said plainly. Since October 28, 2024, U.S. Department of Transportation rules give you a cash refund if the airline cancels your flight or delays it 3 hours or more and you choose not to fly, and a bag fee refund if your bag is more than 12 hours late on a domestic flight. Bluewren tells you when they apply.
      4. Nothing changes without you. Changes and refunds are approved in the Bluewren app on your phone through a URL elicitation; refunds are also confirmed by voice first through a form elicitation. A client that can't ask must call twice with a confirmation id.
      5. Alexa+ does the rest. Bluewren returns your new arrival time and hotel. Alexa uses them with its own services: texting whoever is picking you up, an Uber to the hotel, moving a meeting. In this demo those are simulated.

      What's real and what's simulated

      • Real: the MCP server and protocol features, 28 major U.S. airports with their time zones, and the DOT refund rules.
      • Simulated: Bluewren Air itself (a made-up airline), its flights, seats, cancellations and refunds; and Alexa+, its voice, messages, Uber, Expedia and calendar. A small local model stands in for Alexa+'s understanding.
